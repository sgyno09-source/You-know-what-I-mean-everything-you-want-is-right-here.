> **Important:** This repository provides prompt and visual-workflow templates. It does not grant you rights to any person, photograph, fashion design, album artwork, brand, UI, trademark, font, or other third-party material. You are responsible for checking the source, license scope, platform rules, and applicable law before using or publishing generated work.

> This page is a general risk reminder, not legal advice. Copyright, privacy, likeness, publicity, personality, trademark, advertising, and synthetic-media rules vary by jurisdiction. For commercial or high-risk use, seek qualified professional advice.

---

## 1. Use Reference Images You Have the Right to Use

Prefer reference images that are:

- your own photo
- provided by a person who has clearly consented
- covered by a valid model / image license
- sourced from a library that permits your intended use
- original fictional characters you control

A photo being publicly available online does not automatically mean it is free to reuse.

Avoid using private or unauthorized photos to:

- impersonate a real person
- fabricate endorsements, partnerships, or commercial relationships
- create misleading advertisements
- place someone in sexualized, humiliating, or distressing contexts without consent
- create content likely to damage a person's reputation, employment, or real-world relationships

---

## 2. Real-Person Likeness and Personality Rights

Even when the original photo is not copied pixel-for-pixel, a generated image that strongly reproduces an identifiable real person may still implicate likeness, publicity, personality, privacy, or related rights.

Public figures, celebrities, creators, athletes, and influencers do not automatically lose these rights. Commercial advertising, fake endorsements, and content that implies real participation generally carry more risk.

Good practice:

- obtain explicit permission for commercial use
- do not imply a real person endorsed or participated in a project when they did not
- do not use a real person's likeness to fabricate news, evidence, identity documents, or misleading claims
- follow platform disclosure requirements for generated or synthetic media when applicable

---

## 3. Minors

The people described by this repository's visual templates should be treated as **clearly adult characters** by default.

When minors are involved, use extra care:

- never place minors in sexualized or adult-oriented contexts
- do not use private images of minors for sensitive generation
- do not rely on “looks adult” when the actual age is known to be under 18
- protect school, home, contact, and other identifying information

If age is unclear, use a clearly adult character instead.

---

## 4. Copyright in Photos, Artwork, Fashion, and Other Assets

Photographs, illustrations, posters, typefaces, album covers, character designs, patterns, product images, and other creative assets may be protected by copyright or related rights.

Prefer:

- material you created yourself
- properly licensed assets
- open-license material that permits the intended use
- public-domain material

A style reference should not become a near-copy of a specific protected work, especially for commercial publication.

---

## 5. Album Covers, Music Cards, and Artist Assets

Some templates, especially Skill 05, may include variables such as:

- `{{album artwork}}`
- `{{song title}}`
- `{{artist name}}`
- music-player UI

Keep in mind:

- album artwork is typically copyrighted
- artist photography may involve likeness rights
- branded player interfaces, icons, and visual assets may involve copyright, trademark, or product-design interests
- commercial use should not suggest that a platform, artist, or record label sponsored or approved the project unless that is true

A safer approach is to use original album artwork, fictional music metadata, and a generic player interface.

---

## 6. Mobile UI, Brands, Logos, and Trademarks

Share sheets, incoming-call screens, maps, and music players in this repository are primarily visual-layout concepts.

For public or commercial work, consider:

- using generic or original UI
- avoiding a near-exact copy of a branded interface where it may imply source or affiliation
- avoiding unauthorized logos
- not implying official partnership, endorsement, or sponsorship
- reviewing trademark, trade-dress, and interface-use issues based on the specific project

If you only need a “mobile OS” aesthetic, there is usually no need to reproduce a real brand's interface exactly.

---

## 7. Before Commercial Use

If the output will be used in:

- advertising
- brand campaigns
- e-commerce
- commercial posters
- paid content
- packaging
- client work

check at least the following:

1. Do you have model / likeness permission?
2. Does the source reference image permit commercial use?
3. Are clothing graphics, artwork, album covers, and other assets licensed for the intended use?
4. Are third-party logos, trademarks, fonts, or UI assets present?
5. Does the publishing platform require disclosure of generated or synthetic content?
6. Could an ordinary viewer mistakenly believe that a real person or brand participated in, endorsed, or approved the project?

---

## 8. Privacy and Sensitive Information

Before uploading reference images, remove unnecessary sensitive information where possible, including:

- identity documents
- home addresses
- phone numbers
- license plates
- school or workplace access information
- private chat screenshots
- medical or financial information

Before publishing generated work, inspect the background to make sure such information was not unintentionally preserved.

---

## 9. Recommended Rule of Thumb

A practical rule is:

**Use people you have permission to depict + use assets you have permission to use + do not impersonate real people + do not fabricate endorsements + protect privacy + re-check rights before commercial publication.**

If you are unsure whether a reference photo, album cover, logo, or design can be used, prefer an original or clearly licensed replacement.

---

## 10. Suggested Default Rule for New Skills

When extending this repository, consider keeping this default safeguard:

```text
Characters should be clearly adult by default.
Reference images should be owned by the user or used with valid permission.
Do not use generated output to impersonate real people, fabricate endorsements, invade privacy, or create other unauthorized high-risk uses.
Users are responsible for verifying rights to third-party images, album artwork, logos, trademarks, fonts, and UI assets.
Before commercial use, re-check model releases, asset licenses, platform rules, and applicable law.
```
